# Ahmed's taste: what gets requested or skipped

The daily run reads this before choosing items and rewrites it from the review page's decisions (`decisions` collection). A pattern becomes a rule when at least 5 decisions with reasons back it and none contradict it. Otherwise it stays a hypothesis.

## Hard rules (confirmed)
- Iraq only, cash on delivery. Dropship-suitable items only.
- Exclude clothing and sized items, liquids and cosmetics, fragile items.
- Air freight only: volume weight (L×W×H ÷ 5000) ≤ 2 kg.
- Iraq price = 3 × landed cost, within 35,000–75,000 IQD.
- No profit floor at selection. The ghost test budget averages about $50 per item.
- **No pets items.** Pets aren't a market in Iraq (Ahmed, 2026-11-23). Only 2 pet items were ever ghost-tested.
- **No kids' toys or games unless clearly trending and on-taste** (Ahmed, 2026-11-23). Ghost tests: toys pass 27% of 26 tests vs 31% overall.
- **No near-repeats:** one item per product type. Skip types already shown, like a second pair of binoculars or another hand warmer.

## Evidence from 1,487 Commercial Platform ghost tests (local items, passed vs failed)
Overall pass rate is 31%.
- **Above average:** car items 52% (46 tests); pedestal and rechargeable fans 50%; garment steamers 44%; back support belts 44%; garden hose kits 44%; car accessories 44%; heaters 44%; portable gas stoves 40%; pizza/bread makers 39%; pressure cookers 38%; action cameras 7 of 8.
- **Below average:** electric kettles 25%; air coolers 22%; dash cams 17%; mini washing machines 17%; kitchen organisers 14%; steam cleaners 11%; massage devices 9%; cookware sets 9%; air fryers 0 of 11; phone stands 0 of 9.
- Car items pass well in tests, but Ahmed skipped the visor, dash cam and car vacuum as common or cheap-looking. So car items need to be new to Iraq, not just car-related.

## Learned from reasons (as of 2026-11-22: 12 of 22 decisions have reasons)
Skip reasons (8 skips): saturated 7 · low_value 5 · weak_proof 4 · margin 3 · shipping 2 · no_wow 1.
Request reasons (4 requests): open 3 · value 2 · problem 2 · mass 2 · season 2 · demo 2 · gift 1.

**Rule 1: skip anything already common in Iraq** (7 skips, no request flagged as common).
Generic gadgets that every Iraqi phone, car or home shop already sells get skipped. Examples: flashlights, galaxy projectors, car vacuums, dash cams, door seals, screwdriver sets, cutting boards, air dusters.
My "open in Iraq" scores were too generous: I gave 4/5 to door seal, titanium board, screwdriver cube and sun visor, and all four were skipped as saturated. Score *open* strictly:
- 5: not seen in Iraq at all
- 3: a few pages sell it
- 1: in every shop

Only include items scoring 3 or more.

**Rule 2: skip things that look cheap for 35k+ IQD** (5 skips).
Simple plastic or flat accessories with no visible tech or mechanism look like 5–10k items to an Iraqi buyer. Examples: sun visor, door seal, cutting board, galaxy projector.
Requests had a clear "worth the price" look (`value` on the laser level and dent puller).

*Hypotheses (need more reasons):*
- Weak proof (4) goes with saturated and low value, so it's likely the same signal: generic items with no standout listing.
- Thin margin (3) and shipping risk (2) hit cheap bulky battery items, like the car vacuum and air duster. Battery alone is fine: 6 of 8 requests have batteries.
- Requests combine **not common in Iraq + worth the price + solves a problem or demos well**. Winter timing helps right now (hand warmers, vacuum storage bags).

## From items Ahmed added (inbox, 2026-11-22)
